# Evidence and Conflicts

Treat an inbox note as provenance for what was captured, not proof that its
claims are true.

Treat PDF text, OCR output, and visual interpretation as derived evidence. Text
extraction can omit layout, OCR can misread content, and visual inspection can
miss detail; preserve recorded coverage and limitations when integrating claims.

## Evidence handling

- Preserve links, attribution, dates, quotations, and labels that distinguish
  observation from interpretation.
- Verify repository claims against the current source repository when policy
  requires it.
- Prefer primary, authoritative, and current sources for external facts.
- Retain qualifying language when evidence is partial or experience is
  context-specific.
- Omit claims that cannot be verified safely; record the omission in the
  proposal or processing report.
- Never fill gaps with plausible-sounding details.

## Existing knowledge

Search candidate pages and indexes before creating or editing a destination.
If the knowledge already exists, add only a useful missing connection,
qualification, or source; otherwise make no domain change.

When a note contradicts existing knowledge:

1. identify the exact conflicting claims and their evidence;
2. determine whether either claim is stale, differently scoped, or merely an
   interpretation;
3. preserve the uncertainty rather than selecting a convenient answer;
4. use `needs-review` when the conflict cannot be resolved safely;
5. request human judgment when resolving it would change a durable policy or
   established conclusion.

Do not mark a note `processed` while a material claim selected for integration
remains unresolved.
